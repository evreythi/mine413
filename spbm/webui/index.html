SPBM Stockpile reconstruction & block model

What this is

SPBM reconstructs how truck-dumped material moved through a long-term ore stockpile, and builds a 3-D block model of tonnes, Cu, Au, Ag and contained metal, with an uncertainty range for every number.

The problem

Trucks dump ore at the top of the pile and dozers push it over the edge. Nobody records where each load finally ends up, so the grade inside the pile is unknown.

The data

FMS records say what each truck dumped, where and when.
Survey change solids say where new volume appeared between surveys.

The answer

Each load is matched to the part of the surveyed fill it most plausibly made, with the least dozer push work. Mass and metal are conserved exactly.

What it produces

Block model
5 × 5 × 2 m blocks as CSV (for mine-planning software) and VTK (for ParaView), with grade, tonnes, source loads and confidence class.
Inventory
Tonnes and grade, split into FMS-tracked, unattributed and pre-record material, so measured and assumed material are never mixed.
Uncertainty
Monte Carlo P10 / P50 / P90 ranges, a sensitivity table, and scenarios for interpretations the data cannot rule out.
Audit trail
Hash of every input, the configuration, the code version, and a change report between model versions.

How it works

The surveys decide where volume went, the FMS decides what went there, and a physically based push cost decides which load filled which part of the fill. Full mathematics and all 32 assumptions are in METHOD.md.

The steps

  1. Ingest and check the dataEvery file is fingerprinted (SHA-256) and stored once; raw files are never edited. A catalogue of 40 rules flags bad records and survey problems. Records are flagged, never silently deleted. Corrections go only through config/manual_interventions.csv, each with a reason, author and date.
  2. Line up time and volumeSurvey times are not supplied, so the cut-off hour is scanned to find where FMS volume best matches surveyed volume (result: the next day at 07:00, a shift change). A volume factor converts tonnes to in-pile volume, and is tested on intervals it was not fitted to.
    vi = Ti · fv / Densi     fv = Σ net fill / Σ FMS volume (over consistent intervals)
  3. Build the surfacesEach survey solid is rasterised on a 1 m grid, giving a top and bottom elevation per cell. A floor plane is fitted to the toe, and the surface under the earliest surveys is inferred.
  4. Reconstruct the movement (CPST)For each survey interval: sources are the loads dumped, material cut by dozers, and material carried from earlier; sinks are the cells where the survey shows new fill. The cost of moving a source to a sink is the dozer push distance over the real terrain, with uphill pushing penalised:
    wij = ℓij + α · max(0, zj − zi)     Ckc = shortest-path push distance (≤ 90 m)
    The loads are then shared out over the fill so that the total pushing is as small as possible, with a little mixing allowed (the mixing length ε = 6 m covers GPS error and spill). Mathematically this is an “optimal transport” problem, solved with the Sinkhorn method:
    minP ≥ 0 Σ Pkc Ckc − ε H(P)    subject to   P 1 = a (load volumes),   PT 1 = b (surveyed fill)
    Two extra “bins” keep the books honest: fill that no truck explains is labelled unattributed, and truck volume that finds no fill waits on the platform and is pushed in the next period.
  5. Build the block modelLayers are integrated exactly into 5 × 5 × 2 m blocks. Grades are always metal ÷ tonnes, never averaged. Each block keeps its tonnes by class, its contributing loads and a confidence class (High / Medium / Low / Uninformed).
  6. ValidateMass and metal balance against the FMS; model surface against the survey; a forward physics model tested on survey intervals it never saw; and a synthetic “twin” where the true answer is known.
  7. Quantify uncertaintyA Monte Carlo run re-does the whole reconstruction with GPS error, tonnage error, grade error, survey error, cut-off, mixing length, push penalty and cost form all sampled together. A one-at-a-time sensitivity table and separate scenarios cover what the ranges do not.
  8. Record the audit trailThe run manifest stores input hashes, the configuration, the code version and library versions. Each run is archived, and a change report compares it with the previous version.

Information classes

FMS-tracked measured tonnes and grades, placed by the reconstruction.
Unattributed surveyed fill with no FMS record; grade imputed from loads of the same days.
Pre-record the core of the pile that existed before the record; geometry inferred and grade assumed.

What the data cannot settle

Surveyed volume is about 47% more than the FMS tonnes would fill at their recorded density. That can be bulking, payload under-recording or missing loads, and the data cannot tell these apart. The base case treats it as bulking; the other readings are run as scenarios and reported separately.
